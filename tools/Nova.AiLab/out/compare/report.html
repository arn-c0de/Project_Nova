Reading the feel table. exchange /100 is enemy entities lost per 100 own, -1 when the candidate lost nothing. combat intervals and largest jump describe the shape of the loss curve: many intervals losing one unit each is a trickle, few intervals losing many is a battle — the same total, a very different match. reaction ticks is the mean delay between a unit losing health and that unit being sent somewhere else, -1 when it never happened; unanswered counts the damage that got no answer at all. APM is intents read as a human rate. endings is how many different matches the whole set produced — 1 means the seeds change nothing.

A staging rule is supposed to raise the deciding tick. Waiting is the point of it. Judged on the first table alone it looks like a regression, which is how two earlier changes were read (journal V002, V003, methodology finding M001).

How to read this. Colour marks DEVIATION from the reference row, not goodness — more credits is not better than fewer, a faster decision is not better than a slower one. Nothing here is summed into a score, and nothing is sorted by merit; a single number would reliably reward the wrong behaviour.

Every candidate played both factions against the frozen reference. Alliance and Legion are deliberately asymmetric, and playing both also cancels the spawn-order advantage.

rejected is the underestimated column: it shows where the AI ran into executor rules, which is silent everywhere else.
